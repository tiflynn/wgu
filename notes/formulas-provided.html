01 · Financial Statement Formats

The three core statements, in the exact layout the test screen shows

Balance Sheet Identity
Assets = Liabilities + Owners' Equity
The foundational equation. Liabilities + Owners' Equity are the sources that funded the assets; Assets are what the company has as a result.

Income Statement — flows top to bottom in three stages, shown as one stacked equation:

 Sales
−Cost of Goods Sold
=Gross Profit
−Administrative Expenses
−Depreciation
−Other Expenses
=EBIT (Earnings Before Interest and Taxes)
−Interest
=EBT (Earnings Before Taxes)
−Taxes
=Net Income
Statement of Cash Flows
Operating + Investing + Financing = Net Increase (or Decrease) in Cash
Every cash transaction the company makes falls into exactly one of these three buckets. Sum all three and you get the total change in the cash balance for the period.

03 · DuPont Framework

Breaks Return on Equity into three drivers

DuPont ROE Decomposition
ROE = Return on Sales × Asset Turnover × Assets-to-Equity Ratio
Return on Sales = profitability. Asset Turnover = efficiency. Assets-to-Equity = leverage. Two companies can post the identical ROE for very different reasons — one earned it through strong margins, the other through heavier debt financing. DuPont shows you which.
Notice: the Assets-to-Equity ratio inside this DuPont line IS the "Assets-to-Equity Ratio" listed separately on the additional-formulas sheet (Total Assets ÷ Shareholder's Equity) — same ratio, just embedded here instead of spelled out on its own.

04 · Cash Flow Ratios

Compare reported earnings against actual cash generated

Ratio Formula Interpretation
Cash flow to net income Cash from operationsNet income ≈1.0 = earnings well-supported by cash; <1.0 = possible red flag (earnings not converting to cash)
Cash flow adequacy Cash from operationsCash required for investing activities ≥1.0 = "cash cow," self-funds its own investing needs
Cash times interest earned Cash from operations before interest & taxesCash paid for interest Cash-basis version of Times Interest Earned — how comfortably cash flow covers interest

05 · Activity-Based Costing

The 4-step version as written on the official formula sheet

1
Identify overhead cost activities
2
Analyze overhead costs
in terms of those activities
3
Identify measurable cost drivers
4
Assign overhead
based on the cost drivers
Watch for a step-count mismatch: this official sheet gives ABC in 4 steps. The textbook breaks the same process into 5 steps (splitting "identify activities" and "create cost pools" into two separate steps, and adding a 5th step — "use the information" — at the end). Both are testable phrasings of the same process; know which wording a question is quoting.

06 · Cost-Volume-Profit Analysis

What's actually spelled out on the official sheet

Breakeven Point
Sales − Variable Costs − Fixed Costs = $0
The point where profit is exactly zero. Swap the $0 for a target profit number and this same equation solves for target-income scenarios: Sales − Variable Costs − Fixed Costs = Target Income.
Contribution Margin
Contribution Margin = Sales − Variable Costs
What's left from sales after covering variable costs — available to cover fixed costs, then profit.
Contribution Margin Ratio
Contribution Margin Ratio = Contribution MarginSales
The % of every sales dollar left over after variable costs.

07 · Exam-Trap Cheat Sheet

What this sheet gives you — and what it quietly leaves you to finish yourself

  • The breakeven DIVISION formulas aren't spelled out here. The sheet shows Contribution Margin and Contribution Margin Ratio, but stops short of "Fixed Costs ÷" in front of them. You have to supply that yourself: Units to Break Even = Fixed Costs ÷ CM per unit, and Sales $ to Break Even = Fixed Costs ÷ CM Ratio. (Fully spelled out on the "formulas to memorize" page.)
  • ABC is 4 steps here, 5 in the textbook — same process, different granularity. Don't be thrown if a question's step count doesn't match what you studied.
  • Asset turnover ≠ Fixed Asset Turnover. This sheet's "Asset turnover" uses Total Assets in the denominator. A separate ratio — Fixed Asset Turnover, using only Average Fixed Assets — is NOT on this sheet; it's on the memorize page.
  • Debt ratio ≠ Debt-to-Equity ratio. This sheet's "Debt ratio" divides by Total Assets. Debt-to-Equity (divides by Total Equity instead) is a different ratio, and it's not here — it's on the memorize page.
  • This income statement format is a simplified layout for quick math — it won't show you Discontinued Operations or Extraordinary Items the way the full textbook version does. Don't expect this exact same abbreviated structure to hold for every income-statement question.
