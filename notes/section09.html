01 · Types of Problems in Financial Statements

Topic 8.1 — LO1

  • Three categories, not two: Errors, Disagreements, and Frauds. It's tempting to simplify this to "errors vs. fraud," but Disagreements is its own distinct middle category — a legitimate judgment call is neither a mistake nor a lie.
CategoryWhat it isExamplesPrimary safeguard
Errors Unintentional Wrong amounts, transactions not recorded, duplicate entries, wrong accounts, incorrect physical counts Internal controls (well-designed information system + careful human procedures)
Disagreements Differing judgments & estimates — nobody is "wrong" Useful life of a building, % complete on a long-term contract, A/R collectibility, warranty return rates, employee post-retirement lifespan, likelihood of losing a lawsuit, probability of using future tax benefits Auditors + self-regulation to protect reputation
Frauds Intentional lies To get a bank loan, attract investors, increase a manager's bonus — deceiving employees, suppliers, regulators, or the public Auditors + market punishment + legal enforcement
The Two Types of Fraud
Misappropriation  |  Fraudulent Financial Reporting
Misappropriation = stealing assets (cash, inventory). Fraudulent Financial Reporting = lying within the statements themselves (overstating revenue, hiding liabilities). Misappropriation often then requires falsifying records to cover the theft up.

02 · Internal Controls & the 5 Control Activities

Topic 8.2 — LO2

Internal controls accomplish five objectives: (1) provide accurate accounting records, (2) safeguard assets & records, (3) run operations effectively & efficiently, (4) comply with management policies, (5) comply with laws & regulations.

Note on your own materials: Topic 8.2 describes internal control structure with five categories (Control Environment, Risk Assessment, Control Activities, Information & Communication, Monitoring), while the Topic 8.7 review condenses this to three (control environment, accounting systems, control procedures). Both wordings appear in your notes — know both, since either could show up on the assessment.

The five specific Control Activities (a sub-category of the structure above):

1

Segregation of Duties

"Hands that touch assets do not also touch the accounting records for those assets." Classic example: the cash register separates the cashier (touches cash) from the record (the machine).

2

Proper Authorization

Only approved actions should take place — and only people with the right level of authority can approve them.

3

Physical Control

Locked doors & file cabinets, physical partitions, cameras for remote observation, backup systems for computer records.

4

Adequate Documents & Records

Documents = methods of gathering data from the environment (forms, computer inputs). Records = the accounting system itself.

5

Independent Checks

External auditors, internal auditors, and rotation of duties (including mandatory vacations) — someone eventually looks over your shoulder.

03 · Earnings Management: Why It Happens

Topic 8.3 — LO3

Earnings Management (definition)
Strategic choice of accounting estimates & judgments → to meet pre-determined targets
Companies don't do anything — managers do. It's people making deliberate choices within the flexibility accounting allows.
ReasonWho it's really forExample trigger
Meet internal targetsInternal (the manager, the company)Bonus threshold, sales quota, avoiding a loan covenant violation
Meet external expectationsExternal (investors, the public, creditors)Missing analyst EPS by a penny, or turning a net loss into a net profit for outside readers
Income smoothingExternal (perception of stability)Reporting a steady trend instead of a volatile one — total earnings unchanged, just the pattern
Window dressingExternal (a specific upcoming event)Cleaning up the financials before an IPO or a big loan application
The corrected exam test: don't require a super-specific trigger (an exact bonus dollar figure, an exact analyst estimate) before calling something "internal" or "external." Ask instead: who is this number really being managed for? A net loss turning into a net profit is about outside perception of company health — that's "meeting external expectations," even with no analyst figure or IPO/loan mentioned.

04 · The Earnings Management Continuum & the GAAP Oval

Topic 8.3 — the most-tested concept in this section

Savvy Transaction Timing
→
Aggressive Accounting
(disclosed)
→
Deceptive Accounting
(undisclosed)
→
Fraudulent Reporting
→
Fraud
(fictitious transactions)

GE's income smoothing (real transactions, just timed cleverly) sits at the safe end; inventing transactions or assets outright sits at the fraud end.

The Ethics Test
Was there intent to deceive?
Staying inside the GAAP Oval (technically compliant) does not automatically mean ethical — a manager can pick a GAAP-compliant number specifically to mislead readers without disclosure, and that's still unethical by this test.
Exam trap — the word "always": the continuum has no hard walls between categories; it explicitly blends from one point to the next. A question asking whether certain categories are "always" ethical is testing whether you recognize that there's no firm universal line — not whether you can recite where the line "should" be.

The GAAP Oval: because accounting relies on estimates (useful life, bad debt %, etc.), there isn't one single "correct" earnings number — there's a legitimate range of GAAP-compliant figures. Inside the oval = technically legal. Outside the oval = straightforward fraud. Where you land inside the oval, and whether you disclosed why, is what determines the ethics.

05 · The Sarbanes-Oxley Act (SOX)

Topic 8.4 — LO4

Named for co-sponsors Senator Paul Sarbanes (D-MD) and Representative Michael Oxley (R-OH). Passed in response to the Enron (2001, $80B collapse) and WorldCom (2002) scandals — both involved management colluding to produce deceptive financial reports. Called the most comprehensive financial-reporting legislation since the SEC's creation in 1933.

SOX did three primary things
Created the PCAOB  |  Constrained Auditors  |  Constrained Management
PartyRequirements
PCAOB
(5 full-time members, supervised by the SEC)		 Register all public accounting firms · Establish audit report standards · Conduct inspections of accounting firms · Conduct investigations & discipline inadequate firms · Enforce SOX compliance (no authority to imprison — refers cases onward)
Auditors Prohibited from providing many nonaudit services to audit clients · Audit partners rotate off every 5 years · Report to and are retained by the audit committee, not the CFO/management
Management CEO & CFO must personally certify the financial statements + provide an internal control assessment · Must maintain an officer code of ethics · No loans to executive officers/directors · Must support a stronger, non-management audit committee
Why the Enron detail matters: Enron's auditor received roughly $25M for the audit and another $25M for other (nonaudit) services in the same year — exactly the kind of financial coziness the nonaudit-services rule was written to eliminate.

06 · Internal vs. External Auditors

Topic 8.5 — LO5

Internal AuditorsExternal Auditors
WhoFull-time company employees, independent of day-to-day accountingIndependent CPAs, retained (not employed) by the company
Reports toBoth top management and the audit committee — a channel around management if management itself is the problemThe audit committee (per SOX), not the CFO
CertifiesN/A — evaluates controls, detects fraud, monitors recordsThat statements are presented fairly, in accordance with GAAP (following GAAS while doing so)
Does NOT certify—That statements are 100% free of error (only material misstatements), or that the company is a good investment

Five audit processes — two are external-auditor-only:

1

Interviews Both

Verify procedures are understood, documentation is made, authorization is obtained.

2

Observations Both

Physically view assets — cash, inventory, land, equipment.

3

Sampling Both

Test a statistically reliable subset — auditors can't examine every transaction.

4

Confirmation External only

Contact outsiders directly (banks, customers) to verify a reported balance actually exists.

5

Analytical Procedures External only

Comparative ratio analysis to flag relationships that don't make sense (e.g., A/R rising while sales fall).

Auditor independence is reinforced by three forces: the law (SOX), litigation risk (lawsuits can run into the hundreds of millions), and reputation (an audit firm's most valuable asset).

07 · Materiality & GAAS vs. GAAP

Concepts discussed alongside Topic 8.5

Materiality
Reasonable assurance against MATERIAL misstatement — not a guarantee of zero error
Auditors aren't hunting every small error — only ones big enough to change a reader's decision. Materiality is relative to company size, not a fixed dollar figure: a $50,000 error is likely material for a $200K-revenue company, but immaterial for a $50B one.
AcronymStands forWho follows itGoverns
GAAPGenerally Accepted Accounting PrinciplesThe company (its accountants)How transactions get recorded — the "recipe" for preparing the statements
GAASGenerally Accepted Auditing StandardsThe external auditorHow the audit itself is conducted — the "inspection process" rules
One sentence to memorize: "The auditor conducts the audit in accordance with GAAS, in order to determine whether the financial statements are presented fairly in accordance with GAAP." GAAS = how I checked. GAAP = what I was checking for.

08 · The SEC & Required Filings

Topic 8.6 — LO6

Congress's Commerce Clause authority (Article 1, §8, Clause 3) was delegated to the SEC via the Securities Acts of 1933 & 1934, passed after the deceptive practices behind the 1920s bubble and 1929 crash. Mission: protect investors, maintain fair/orderly/efficient markets, facilitate capital formation — with the accounting-specific slice being to ensure users get reliable information.

The SEC has constitutional authority to set both accounting and auditing rules directly, but in practice delegates day-to-day rule-writing to the FASB (accounting) and the PCAOB (auditing) — while keeping oversight of both.

  • Enforcement powers: set accounting/disclosure rules, suspend trading, de-list a security, suspend brokers/dealers/accountants, investigate violations, impose civil fines.
  • What the SEC can't do: imprison anyone directly — there's no "SEC court." It refers cases to a U.S. district attorney for criminal prosecution.
  • Adds credibility by: requiring independent audits, reviewing financial statements itself, and sanctioning firms that violate its standards.

09 · Exam-Trap Cheat Sheet

The mix-ups that show up most often

  • Errors / Disagreements / Frauds — three categories, not two. A judgment call (disagreement) isn't the same as a mistake (error) or a lie (fraud).
  • Form 10-Q is NOT formally audited — only the annual Form 10-K is. The 10-Q gets CPA involvement/review, not a full audit.
  • Confirmation & Analytical Procedures are external-auditor-only — Interviews, Observations, and Sampling are used by both internal and external auditors.
  • GAAS vs. GAAP — GAAS is what the auditor follows while auditing; GAAP is what the company follows while recording transactions and what the auditor is checking for.
  • "Always ethical" questions on the continuum — be suspicious of absolutes. The continuum has no hard walls, so "there's no clear line" often beats naming a specific "safe" category.
  • Meeting external expectations vs. window dressing — window dressing is tied to a specific known event (an IPO or loan application). If no such event is mentioned, but the scenario is about how outsiders will perceive the company (e.g., loss vs. profit), it's "meeting external expectations" instead.
  • Materiality is relative, not a fixed dollar figure — always ask "material relative to what?" (company size, context) before judging a dollar amount.
  • Misappropriation vs. Fraudulent Financial Reporting — misappropriation = stealing assets; fraudulent financial reporting = lying in the statements themselves.
  • The audit committee must be non-management — it's a subset of the board made up specifically of outside directors, so it can act as an independent channel even if top management is the source of the problem.
  • Internal control structure: 5 categories vs. 3 — Topic 8.2 uses 5 (Control Environment, Risk Assessment, Control Activities, Information & Communication, Monitoring); the Topic 8.7 review condenses this to 3 (control environment, accounting systems, control procedures). Know both wordings.
