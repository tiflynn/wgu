Same destination, different route: this layout nets Interest Income and Interest Expense as two separate lines instead of one combined "Interest" line, and folds admin costs into one "Selling and Administrative Expenses" line rather than splitting out Depreciation and Other Expenses. Whichever version a question uses, the core skeleton is identical: Sales → Gross Profit → Operating Income → Net Income, moving from most-core operations to least.

03 · Activity-Based Costing — Activity Rate

The core rate calculation used in every ABC overhead-assignment problem

Activity Rate (per Cost Pool) Memorize
Activity Rate = Cost PoolCost Driver
This is the workhorse formula behind ABC. Each overhead cost pool (e.g., all costs tied to machine setups) gets divided by its cost driver (e.g., number of setups) to get a rate per unit of driver activity. Multiply that rate by how much of the driver a specific product actually used, and you've assigned that pool's overhead to the product.

04 · CVP — Full Formula Set

Splitting mixed costs, plus the complete breakeven & target-profit toolkit, fully spelled out

High-Low Method — Variable Cost per Unit Memorize
Variable Cost per Unit = Highest Cost − Lowest CostHighest Activity − Lowest Activity
Splits a mixed cost into its variable and fixed pieces using only the highest- and lowest-activity months — pick the rows by activity level, not by dollar amount.
High-Low Method — Fixed Cost Memorize
Fixed Cost = Total Cost − (Variable Cost per Unit × Activity Level)
Plug either the high or the low row back in — both give the same fixed cost.

Same shape as the plain breakeven formula — just add the target profit into the numerator before dividing.
Sales Dollars to Earn Desired Profit Memorize

Multi-Product Break-Even Memorize
Break-Even Sales $ = Total Fixed CostsWeighted-Average Contribution Margin Ratio
With more than one product, weight each product's CM ratio by its share of the sales mix. Change the mix and break-even moves, even if no price or cost changed.

05 · Exam-Trap Cheat Sheet

Don't confuse these with their similar-sounding cousins on the provided sheet

On the PROVIDED sheetOn THIS pageDon't mix them up
Debt ratio=LiabilitiesAssets Debt-to-Equity Ratio=LiabilitiesEquity Same numerator, different denominator — Assets vs. Equity
Asset turnover=SalesTotal Assets Fixed Asset Turnover=SalesAverage Fixed Assets Total assets vs. only fixed assets, and note the word "Average"
DuPont's Assets-to-Equity pieceembedded in ROE formula Assets-to-Equity Ratio=Total AssetsEquity These are the same ratio — not a trap, just a redundant listing
  • The breakeven formulas here ARE complete — unlike the provided sheet (which stops at "Contribution Margin Ratio" and expects you to add "Fixed Costs ÷" yourself), this page spells the full division out. Use this page as the source of truth for breakeven math.
  • Activity Rate = Cost Pool / Cost Driver shows up in nearly every ABC calculation — memorize this one first among everything on this page.
  • "Average" matters. Fixed Asset Turnover, Average Collection Period, and Days in Sales Inventory all use an average balance (typically (beginning + ending) / 2) rather than a single point-in-time figure — don't plug in an ending balance alone unless that's all you're given.
  • Target-profit formulas are breakeven formulas with an extra term. Add Desired Profit into the numerator alongside Fixed Costs, then divide exactly like you would for plain breakeven.
