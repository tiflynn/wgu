01 · Quality Management: QA vs QC

Quality is a culture, and it needs both prevention and detection

  • Quality is a culture. Purchasing, manufacturing, customer service and talent management each own the quality of their process, with a customer focus.
  • An effective program uses both quality assurance (QA) and quality control (QC). Leaders define and communicate the vision and goals.
  • Every member must at least identify and communicate defective product, inefficiencies and irregularities.
  • Senior leadership commitment leads to fact-based decisions, which reduce bias and build trust in plans.
Quality control (QC)Quality assurance (QA)
FocusUncover defects so they can be fixedPrevent defects from occurring at all
PurposeAssess performance, recommend corrective actionAssess process capability, recommend preventive action
ResponseReactive (after the problem)Proactive (before the problem)
LevelBasic: recognize problems so they can be fixedAdvanced: understand the process well enough to predict outcomes
Major activityInspection of results to find defectsTraining, standards, procedures
Inspection wording: the course text lists "training and inspections" as QA examples (such as testing an update before launch), while the Express session calls inspection QC's major activity. Decide by timing: preventing a defect before it exists is QA; finding or fixing one that already exists is QC.
  • Walter Shewhart began quality control in 1931; the charts were first called Shewhart control charts.
  • W. Edwards Deming promoted control charts and statistical process control after World War II. When few Americans listened, he went to Japan, and Japanese quality rose.

02 · PDCA: Plan-Do-Check-Act

The problem-solving and pilot-test cycle

1 · Plan
→
2 · Do
→
3 · Check
→
4 · Act
StepWhat happensKeyword
PlanInvestigate the cause, then plan a solutioninvestigate
DoTry the solution on a small scaletrial, try
CheckIs the problem still occurring? If yes, loop back to Planstill spoiled? is it reduced?
ActInstitutionalize the solution that worked so it cannot recurmake permanent, standardize
  • Plan, Do and Check parallel QC (fix a problem that exists). Act parallels QA (prevent it from returning).
  • Unknown cause (a customer reports defects)? Start with PDCA's investigation, not QC. QC assumes the problem is already identified.
  • PDSA replaces Check with Study. PDCA is for day-to-day operations; PDSA leads toward a Six Sigma approach.
  • In Lean Six Sigma, PDCA is the pilot-test cycle used in the Improve phase.
  • "Keep an eye on" a process so no problems occur = QA, even if the tool is a control chart.

03 · The Seven Basic Quality Tools

They solve roughly 90 to 95% of quality problems

ToolQuestion it answersKeywordRemember
Run chartHow is performance changing?over timeLine chart; x-axis is always time. Parts: data points, median line, trend, shift.
Fishbone (cause-and-effect, Ishikawa)Why is it happening?possible causesProblem at the head; bones = people, machines, methods, materials, measurement, environment.
FlowchartWhere in the process?steps, orderMaps steps and decisions. SIPOC is a high-level flowchart.
Check sheetHow many times?collect, count, tallyCollects data; can be keyed into a spreadsheet.
HistogramHow is the data distributed?distributedBars = frequency per category; tallest in the middle, like a bell curve.
Pareto chartWhich causes matter most?prioritize, 80/20Histogram sorted tallest to shortest; fix the biggest bar first.
Scatter diagramAre two variables related?relationshipStrength of association (R²); not significance and not cause.
  • A control chart is a run chart plus an upper and lower control limit.
  • Histogram: when the mean, median and mode are close together, the shape is a normal (bell) curve.
  • Pareto principle: about 80% of problems come from about 20% of causes.
  • Read the keyword: "over time" = run chart; "distributed" = histogram; "prioritize" = Pareto.

04 · Statistical Process Control and Control Charts

Telling normal variation from a real problem

Control limits (3 sigma)
UCL = mean + 3 SD  ·  LCL = mean − 3 SD
By the empirical rule, about 99.7% of points fall inside, so only about 0.3% (3 in 1,000) should fall outside. Example: mean 50, SD 4 gives LCL 38 and UCL 62.
Common cause variationSpecial cause variation
Also calledRandom, natural, normal fluctuationNon-random, assignable cause, systematic error
On the chartPoints inside the limitsPoints outside the limits
ResponseExpected; do not tamperFind and correct the cause
Reading a chart: if 3 of 12 points (25%) fall outside 3-sigma limits, that is far above the 0.3% expected, so the process is out of control.
Working backward: if the mean is 16, UCL 17 and LCL 15, the gap of 1 equals 3 SD, so 1 SD = 1/3. Variance is the square of the SD (1/9); the SD is the square root of the variance.
Data typeMeaningExample
Variable dataNumerical; shows exactly where you are in a rangeWeight in grams, minutes to finish
Attribute dataCategorical; shows whether you met the targetPass or fail, on time yes or no
  • SPC is used in the Measure and Control phases, and in Improve to verify a change worked.

Lee Iacocca's five quality points (from the Express session)

PointMeaningIdea

Eight Lean wastes (DOWNTIME): Defects, Overproduction, Waiting, Non-utilized (unused) talent, Transportation, Inventory (excess), Motion, Extra processing.
Typo alert: the Lean Six Sigma video narration says "3.4 defects per billion". The correct figure is per million. Any effort to reduce variation and defects counts as Six Sigma, even before the 3.4 target is reached.

DMAIC and where the tools fit

PhaseQuestionCommon tools
DefineWhat is the problem, scope and process?SIPOC, flowchart
MeasureWhat is current performance?Check sheet, run chart, histogram, Pareto, SPC
AnalyzeWhy is it happening?Fishbone, Pareto, scatter diagram, flowchart
ImproveDoes the fix work?PDCA pilot, then run chart, histogram, Pareto, scatter or check sheet to confirm
ControlAre the gains holding?Statistical process control (control charts)
ISO vs. Lean Six Sigma: ISO is not a Lean Six Sigma method. ISO standardizes and audits how work is managed (process adherence); Lean Six Sigma improves how work is done (process improvement). A certified exporter lets importers use acceptance sampling instead of heavy inspection.

07 · Easy to Confuse and Exam Tips

Read each question twice and underline the keyword

Mix-upHow to tell them apart
QC vs. QACorrective and reactive vs. preventive and proactive. "Keep an eye on" or "make sure there are no problems" = QA.
PDCA vs. QCCause unknown? Start with PDCA's investigation. QC assumes you already know the problem.
Do vs. Check vs. ActTrial = Do. "Is it reduced?" = Check. Make permanent = Act.
Run chart vs. control chart"Over time" alone = run chart. Add UCL and LCL = control chart.
Histogram vs. Pareto"Distributed" = histogram. "Prioritize" or "highest first" = Pareto.
Fishbone vs. flowchartFishbone asks why; flowchart asks where in the process.
Check sheet vs. histogramThe check sheet collects and counts; the histogram displays the counts.
Common vs. special causeInside the limits = random and expected. Outside = assignable, fix it.
Variable vs. attribute dataNumbers on a scale vs. yes/no categories.
Lean vs. Six Sigma vs. JITWaste vs. variation and defects vs. inventory. JIT is one example of lean.
ISO vs. Lean Six SigmaISO audits adherence; Lean Six Sigma improves the process.
  • Express practice answers: "reduce defects per batch" = Six Sigma; "how defects are distributed among teams" = histogram; "are defects reducing over time" = run chart.
  • Keyword strategy: read the question straight through for context, then again starting from the question itself to find the keyword.
