01 · Performance Measures and Critical Success Factors

You cannot improve a process you do not measure

  • Performance starts with setting goals (financial, safety or process). Leadership must clearly communicate them so activities can be put in place to achieve them.
  • Performance measurement = collecting data and applying analytics to monitor progress and stimulate improvement toward the goals.
  • The set goals are the critical success factors (CSFs): what must go well. A KPI is the metric that measures one of them.
  • Not every measure is financial (safety, loyalty, process quality), but they usually affect the bottom line.
TermMeaningGeneric example
Performance measuresMetrics used to track and improve performance against goalsAny metric tied to a goal
Critical success factorA goal or area that must go well for the organization to succeedGrowing the customer base
KPIThe specific metric that measures one targetNew customers each month
Valid measures need good data. Collect relevant data from multiple sources: financial statements, sales figures, customer feedback, employee performance metrics, production data and market research. Accurate, reliable data keeps measurements objective, consistent and true to operations.

02 · Results-Based Management (RBM)

Express session: ongoing, team based, focused on results

  • Life cycle approach. The phrase life cycle in a question usually means RBM.
  • Team based (partnership). Involves everyone who can influence the outcome, even the night cleaning staff.
  • Long-term impacts. Every action aligns with measurable, sustainable outcomes.
  • Simplified indicators. They streamline evaluation and make progress and accountability easier to track.
  • Keywords: results, life cycle, partnership, ongoing.

03 · KPIs, KPI Dashboards and SMART

One KPI measures one target; a dashboard shows many

  • KPI = a measure of one target of performance. Organizations may have dozens, hundreds or thousands.
  • KPI dashboard = a visual of the performance of a collection of KPIs (two or more), so decision makers track them together.
  • Used by employees, supervisors and managers to improve daily operations in their own area (tactics). Each KPI contributes to overall performance, measured with the balanced scorecard.

04 · The Balanced Scorecard and Variance

Four perspectives kept level against long-term strategy

  • The balanced scorecard (BSC) measures performance in four perspectives, remembered as FLIC.
  • It gauges long-term performance in alignment with the company's vision and strategic growth, not only financial outcomes.
  • Think of a table: if one leg is shorter, it wobbles. Chase profit alone and customers leave; chase happy customers alone and the money runs out. The BSC keeps every leg level.
PerspectiveWhat it asksExample measures
F FinancialHow are the finances?Profitability, cost control, revenue growth
L Learning and growthAre employees up to date and innovating?Training, skill development, innovation
I Internal processesAre processes efficient?Streamlining, launching a new product
C Customer satisfactionAre customers happy?Satisfaction scores, retention
BSC advantagesBSC disadvantages
Internal and external communicationTime and cost to build and maintain
Links operations and performance to corporate strategyLimited: may not display the full picture of performance
Aligns departments with the same overarching goalsMay not encourage desired behavior if metrics are poorly aligned
Emphasizes strategy and organizational resultsHard to roll out across diverse departments
Variance
Variance = actual performance − target performance
A positive variance means the target was beaten (favorable); a negative variance means it was missed (unfavorable). Example: target +1% readers per month. Actual +2% gives +1 (favorable); actual +0.5% gives −0.5 (unfavorable).

05 · Net Promoter Score (NPS)

Customer loyalty from one question: would you recommend us?

  • Ask customers how likely they are to recommend the product or service. The NPS measures customer loyalty.
  • Promoters rate 9 or 10. Passives rate 7 or 8 (satisfied but unlikely to persuade others). Detractors rate 6 or below and are likely to voice negative opinions.
  • Passives are not in the formula. A negative NPS is possible and signals an immediate need to improve customer experience.
  • The average NPS is 20 to 30, but some industries score much higher. Know what is standard for your industry and focus on detractors' feedback.
Net Promoter Score
NPS = % promoters − % detractors
Steps: ask → sort → percent → subtract. Worked example: 500 customers, 280 promoters (56%), 160 passives (32%), 60 detractors (12%). NPS = 56 − 12 = 44, well above the 20 to 30 average.
Express decision example: industry NPS is 15. The company launches a marketing campaign only if its NPS is higher; otherwise it improves the experience of current customers. With 30% promoters and 20% detractors, NPS = 10, below 15, so focus on keeping current customers.
Wording slips to ignore: in the Express recording the formula is said backward (subtract promoters from detractors); the correct formula and the course example are promoters minus detractors. The text's scale is 1 to 10 while the recording says 0 to 10; detractors are 6 or below either way.
  • Benchmarks quoted in the Express session, for flavor only: WGU 68, Harvard Business School 53, Walmart 80, Comcast −26.

06 · Choosing the Right Tool

Match the keywords in the question to the measure

If the question says...Choose
One goal or indicator, such as revenue onlyKPI
Several KPIs shown together visuallyKPI dashboard
Four perspectives, long-term strategy, balance, FLICBalanced scorecard
Likely to recommend, loyalty, promoters and detractorsNet Promoter Score
Ongoing, life cycle, results, partnershipResults-based management
Start at the question mark and work backward. A scenario may list revenue, customer satisfaction and efficiency, but if the question asks how to assess only revenue, the answer is a KPI. If it asked about all three together (financial, customer and process), it would be the balanced scorecard. Then cross out the two answers that obviously do not fit.

07 · Easy to Confuse and Exam Tips

Read the question mark first, then the scenario

Mix-upHow to tell them apart
CSF vs. KPICSF = what must go well. KPI = the number that measures it.
KPI vs. dashboardOne KPI measures one target. A dashboard displays two or more KPIs.
Dashboard vs. BSCDashboard displays KPIs for daily operations (tactics). BSC balances four perspectives against long-term strategy.
Outputs vs. outcomes vs. impactImmediate products vs. short-term effects vs. long-term sustainable change.
Promoters vs. passives vs. detractors9 to 10, 7 to 8, 6 or below. Only promoters and detractors enter the formula.
NPS order of subtractionPromoters minus detractors, never the reverse.
Variance signActual minus target. Positive is favorable, negative is unfavorable.
RBM vs. BSCRBM follows inputs to impact over the life cycle. BSC uses four FLIC perspectives.
SMART vs. FLICSMART = what a good KPI must be. FLIC = the four balanced scorecard perspectives.
  • Keyword strategy: life cycle or ongoing results = RBM; one indicator = KPI; four perspectives = BSC; recommend = NPS.
  • Memory hooks: FLIC (Financial, Learning and growth, Internal processes, Customer); SMART (Specific, Measurable, Attainable, Relevant, Time bound); Inputs, Activities, Outputs, Outcomes, Impact.
  • Calculations: variance = actual − target; NPS = % promoters − % detractors. Convert counts to percentages before subtracting.
  • Watch for bait answers: a promoters-only percentage or a reversed subtraction will be among the choices.
